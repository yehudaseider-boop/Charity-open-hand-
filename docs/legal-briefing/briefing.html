1. Summary for busy readers

What Givezy is. A website and phone app where people give to registered South African charities. The donor pays by card on the payment provider's own page (Paystack, a South African payment provider). The gift is split by the provider and goes straight to the charity's own bank account. Givezy also issues the yearly section 18A tax receipt for a charity that has signed a mandate, and prepares the file the charity itself sends to SARS (the IT3(d) report). Givezy has no registered company yet.

Where we are. Test site and test database only. One test donation has been paid end to end in the provider's test mode (02/11/2026). Declined card, monthly giving and refund tests are still to do. Email sending is not set up: emails are only written to a log. The phone app sends people to the website to give, because in-app giving is switched off until Apple and Google confirm in writing.

The money flow in six lines

  1. The donor chooses a charity and an amount (minimum R30) and pays by card on the provider's page. Givezy never sees card details.
  2. The donor may add a separate contribution to Givezy (unticked by default, minimum R10). Givezy charges no fee on donations.
  3. The provider splits the payment: the gift goes to the charity's own bank account through a "subaccount"; the contribution stays with Givezy's provider account. Nothing is pooled.
  4. The provider's own charge is carried by the charity, out of its gift. The provider has not yet given us its rates, so we show no figure.
  5. A gift counts as paid only when the provider itself confirms the exact amount in rand. A refund or a dispute is held for a person to decide, except a full refund, which marks the gift refunded.
  6. After each tax year ends (1 March to the end of February) Givezy issues one s18A receipt per donor per charity, and the charity reports to SARS itself.

The eight questions that matter most

  1. Q3.1 Does the way we run payments need a licence or registration? Q3.5 and Q3.6: the proof-of-concept structure (the founder as sole-proprietor merchant) and the provider's ZAR 1 000 000 cap.
  2. Q4.1 and Q4.2: can a platform issue s18A receipts under a signed mandate, and what must the mandate say?
  3. Q4.4 and Q4.5: the exact receipt wording and particulars, and whether the full ID number must print (today only the last 4 digits).
  4. Q4.9: does a card charge the donor chooses to cover form part of the deductible amount?
  5. Q5.1 and Q5.3: which SARS Nature of Person a non-profit company, trust or association uses, and whether SARS expects an interim IT3(d) from a charity that receipts once a year.
  6. Q6.1, Q6.6 and Q6.3: responsible party or operator, what we keep after erasure, and the rules for charities emailing their donors.
  7. Q3.9 and Q3.7: who bears refunds and chargebacks, and cooling-off or refund rules for donations and monthly donations.
  8. Q8.1 and Q8.2: VAT and the tax treatment of Givezy's own income (donor contributions).

3.2 What Givezy receives, and when

  • Only the optional contribution. It is a flat amount per payment and arrives in Givezy's provider account. It is never part of the charity's line, never on a receipt, and the charity never sees it.

3.6 The dedication, message and label fields

A donor can type three pieces of free text with a gift: a private label (up to 80 characters; only the donor sees it), a dedication ("in honour of" or "in memory of" and a name of up to 80 characters), and a message to the charity (up to 500 characters). The donor and the charity see the dedication and the message; they are never printed on an s18A receipt and they are wiped when the donor asks for erasure. We found no public page that shows a donor's name, message or dedication. "Give anonymously" hides the donor's name from public pages, campaign lists and live totals, but not from the charity.

It is published as soon as it is posted (profile content is checked at approval), and Givezy administrators can remove any of it. 4.1 What Givezy issues, and the switch

For a charity that a platform administrator has confirmed as s18A-approved and that has a signed mandate, Givezy issues one receipt per donor for all paid gifts in the tax year where the donor ticked "I want an s18A receipt", including gifts the charity recorded by hand (EFT, cash, debit orders, imports). The receipt amount is the sum of the gift amounts only, worked out inside the database, never passed in. Real receipts are switched off: the issuing job refuses to write anything until two settings (RECEIPTS_ENABLED=yes and RECEIPT_WORDING_CONFIRMED=yes) are set, so until Yosef confirms the wording every receipt is a marked draft. The first real run would be March 2027, for the 2026/27 tax year.

Where to check: src/config/receipts.ts, supabase/migrations/20261009000003_issue_receipts.sql, docs/launch-plan.md

4.2 The mandate: how it is recorded

  • A charity that holds an s18A reference uploads the SARS s18A approval letter and the signed receipting mandate, with the date it was signed. Givezy sends the mandate to the charity to sign; there is no template in the repository.
  • A platform administrator (with the second step) checks the documents and ticks "s18A confirmed" and "mandate confirmed". The system then stores the mandate's signing date (midnight Johannesburg time of that date) and the file's location. A charity cannot set these fields itself: a database rule refuses it.
  • A charity can issue only when it is s18A-confirmed and has a mandate date. A gift paid before the mandate date is never receipted.
  • The administrator can also set up s18A later for a charity approved without it; the charity is then emailed.

Where to check: src/app/admin/charities/[id]/actions.ts, src/app/charity-admin/[id]/application/actions.ts, supabase/migrations/20261007000001_charity_onboarding.sql, src/lib/receipts/plan.ts (charityCanIssue)

4.3 When, how often, and numbering

  • Once a year per donor, after the tax year ends. The tax year runs from 1 March to the end of February and is named by the year it ends: 2026/27 is 01/03/2026 to 28/02/2027 (stored as 2027, as SARS numbers it). The job refuses to run before the year has ended, and a charity can approve only the last two closed years.
  • Two approvals. The charity checks and approves its list (donor, number of gifts, amount, total) on its dashboard; Givezy then signs off. If the list changed in between (a refund, a late payment), nothing is issued and it goes back to the charity. A fingerprint of the list is compared.
  • Numbering. CODE-2027-0042: the charity's short code, the tax year, and a four-digit running number that is sequential per charity. The number is taken from a counter inside the same database transaction that writes the receipt and is never reused; a withdrawn receipt keeps its number.
  • Delivery. The PDF is emailed to the donor (once email is set up) and is available in the donor's account and app. A donor's "tax pack" is a separate summary marked as not a tax receipt.
  • A gift confirmed after the receipt was issued is left for a person: withdraw the receipt and issue it again with the late gift on it.

4.5 Nature of donation, and a known weakness

For online gifts the nature of donation prints "Card (online)". For gifts a charity recorded by hand it prints the method it recorded: "EFT (bank transfer)", "Cash", "Debit order", "Another giving programme" or "Other". The weakness: every online gift is printed as "Card (online)" and the text is frozen in the receipt. That is true while the provider takes cards only; it would be wrong for life if instant EFT or debit orders are added online. The fix (storing the method on the gift when it is confirmed) is not built.

Where to check: src/lib/receipts/details.ts (donationNature), src/lib/charity/recorded-gifts.ts (RECORDED_METHODS), docs/go-live-checklist.md (Receipt "nature of donation")

4.6 Donor ID and tax numbers, and the covered card charge

  • At checkout a donor who ticks s18A must give an SA ID number (13 digits, a real date, check digit) and an address; an income tax number is optional (checked with SARS's Modulus 10 rule). A company gives its CIPC registration number and a trust its trust number. The full numbers are encrypted (AES-256-GCM) with a key held outside the database; only the last 4 digits are stored in plain text.
  • On the receipt only the last 4 digits of an ID or tax number print; a company or trust registration number prints in full. Printing full numbers would mean decrypting at issue time and keeping them in the receipt: see Q4.5.
  • The card charge a donor covers (when the provider's rates exist) is paid to the charity's line, but the receipt sums gift amounts only. This was built that way because a covered charge is not a gift; it is a question for you: Q4.9.

4.7 Void and re-issue

  • An issued receipt cannot be edited or deleted. A database rule allows only two changes: attaching the PDF once, and withdrawing it with a reason.
  • A platform administrator withdraws a receipt with a written reason of at least five characters; the audit log records who and why. The gifts on it then go back on the charity's list, and a replacement is issued after the charity approves and Givezy signs off.
  • A full refund or chargeback withdraws the receipt automatically (3.3).
  • Weaknesses: a withdrawn receipt sends the donor no notice, so the PDF already in their inbox still looks valid; a replacement is not automatically linked to the withdrawn receipt; a part-refunded gift keeps its whole amount on the receipt.

5. IT3(d) report to SARS

Written mainly for the accountant. A charity that issues section 18A receipts must report each receipt to SARS on a return called IT3(d). Givezy does not submit anything to SARS. It prepares the data for each charity, and each charity (or its accountant) submits it.

5.1 What SARS requires

  • Documents we followed: SARS External BRS "IT3 Data Submission", version 4.0.0D-10 of 07/10/2022 (272 pages), and the eFiling guide GEN-ENR-01-G03 Revision 6 (41 pages, effective 07/04/2026). Our reading of both, page by page, is in two files in the repository. The BRS PDF has tracked changes: struck-through rules still print as live text, so every IT3(d) page was checked visually and deleted rules were not implemented.
  • Who reports: entities that received donations and issued receipts report those receipts. Where a charity received no donations a "nil reporting declaration" is required (Guide p.4).
  • How: the guide describes a cycle of "bi-annual and annual" submissions (Guide p.3), but neither document states IT3(d) due dates. Up to 50 IT3(d) certificates can be typed into the IT3-01 form on eFiling (Guide p.14; the guide also says 20 in one place, p.4). Larger submissions use a data file sent by HTTPS or Connect Direct, which needs the organisation to be enrolled and activated for third-party data on eFiling, and a 144-character Source Identifier from SARS. The declaration (IT3-02) is made at the submitting entity's level.
  • Test environment: the BRS has a test indicator and says SARS publishes a test Source Identifier on its website; the guide does not describe the test environment.

5.3 Who submits, and the twice-a-year cycle

  • Each charity submits its own file. It is the submitting entity and the only reporting entity in its file (decision of 02/11/2026). Givezy is not a submitting entity, and no charity has yet been enrolled for third-party data.
  • Due dates: what we know and do not know. Our understanding is an interim IT3(d) for receipts issued 1 March to 31 August, due 31 October, and an annual one for the full tax year, due 31 May, plus a nil declaration when nothing was issued. This comes from accounting-firm and PBO-support sources. SARS's own page could not be reached from where we work, and the BRS and the guide do not state it. Please confirm the 2026/27 dates.
  • The consequence for us. Givezy issues receipts once a year, after the tax year ends. So the interim file (first six months) is empty and, if SARS expects a nil declaration, it is made on eFiling, not with a file; the page says so. The alternative is receipting twice a year, which would need a mid-year receipt run. That is undecided (Q5.3).
  • Nil declarations. With no donations no file can be built (the trailer's total must be at least R0.01), so a nil declaration is made on eFiling.

5.4 Donor identification, old receipts and privacy

  • An SA ID number is now required for s18A (decision of 03/11/2026). A tax number alone is not accepted by SARS: the donor identification field is mandatory and the "internal number" type is not allowed (BRS p.225). An individual gives an SA ID number (income tax number optional); a company its CIPC number; a trust its trust number.
  • Old receipts with only a tax number cannot go in the file and show as blocking problems. Whether to report them by hand on eFiling or leave them out is undecided (Q5.9).
  • Monthly donors who set up an s18A monthly gift before 03/11/2026 with only a tax number: each new monthly gift copies those details, so their receipts would keep showing as a problem. We chose not to remove s18A from their gifts; instead a notice and an SA ID field appear in that monthly donation's card on their account page, with an "ask first" confirmation. An email nudge is not built because email sending is not set up.
  • Privacy consequence. The charity's owner downloads full ID numbers for SARS and for that purpose only; the page shows only the last 4 digits. Draft wording added to the Privacy Policy on 03/11/2026 says so, and a home page card says "Your ID stays off the dashboard"; both await Yosef, and the charity terms need a line that the charity keeps those files safe and uses them only for SARS.
  • A donor who asked for erasure cannot be put in a file; their receipt shows as a blocking problem to report from the charity's own records.

7.2 How a charity is checked before it is listed

  1. Invitation or application. A platform administrator can invite a charity by email (a link that works once, for 30 days), or the charity applies on the website. Sign-in is by emailed code, then the Terms agreement and a second step with an authenticator app.
  2. The application asks for: the organisation's names, an NPO or PBO number (at least one), its registered address, a contact person, and a bank account in the organisation's name (the account number is encrypted). Documents: a bank confirmation letter always; a PBO approval letter if it has a PBO number; and, to issue s18A receipts, the SARS s18A approval and the signed receipting mandate.
  3. Four declarations are ticked on submitting: the person is authorised, the information is true, the terms are accepted, and donor information will be handled under POPIA. They are stored with the person and the charity.
  4. Review by hand by a platform administrator (second step), promised within 2 working days: the registration, the bank confirmation letter and, for s18A, the approval and mandate. Approval marks the bank details verified. What will go public is shown on the review page.
  5. Bank check with the provider (information only; it never approves anything). Built, not yet proven It has not been confirmed that it works for a business account without a company registration number.
  6. Connecting to the provider. An administrator creates the charity's payout account; only then can it take donations. A change to the bank details disconnects the charity and clears "verified".
  7. After approval an administrator can suspend or reinstate a charity (the contact is emailed and the page is hidden; no donations; monthly gifts not collected; history kept), remove a person's access, and reset a lost second step after calling a number already on file (logged, and the person is emailed). A rename, or a new logo or cover, is allowed but logged and emailed to the team.
  8. Not built or not decided Not built: sanctions screening, automatic register lookups (NPO, PBO, company), and a second reviewer. Approval is one administrator.

Appendix D. Glossary

TermMeaning
s18ASection 18A of the Income Tax Act, 1962: a donor's tax deduction for gifts to an approved public benefit organisation, claimed with a receipt.
PBOPublic benefit organisation: a charity approved by SARS for tax purposes.
NPO / NPCNon-profit organisation / non-profit company: forms in which many South African charities are registered.
Association not for gainA kind of organisation SARS lists as its own category of entity.
IT3(d)The return a section 18A organisation sends SARS listing the receipts it issued, donor by donor.
BRSSARS's Business Requirement Specification: the technical document that defines the IT3(d) file. We followed version 4.0.0D-10 of 07/10/2022.
eFilingSARS's online filing service. Up to 50 IT3(d) certificates can be typed into a form there; larger submissions use a data file.
Modulus 10A check-digit rule SARS uses to test tax reference numbers.
Submitting entity / reporting entityThe party that sends the file to SARS / the s18A organisation the file is about. In our files both are the charity.
Source IdentifierA 144-character code SARS issues to an enrolled submitter for data-file submission.
Nil declarationA declaration that no s18A receipts were issued in a period.
MandateThe signed document in which a charity authorises Givezy to issue s18A receipts in its name.
POPIAThe Protection of Personal Information Act, 2013.
Responsible party / operatorUnder POPIA, the party that decides why and how personal information is used / a party that processes it for the responsible party.
Information OfficerThe person in an organisation who is answerable under POPIA and must be registered with the Information Regulator.
FICAThe Financial Intelligence Centre Act, 2001: duties on accountable institutions to know their customers and report suspicious transactions.
Split paymentA single card payment that the payment provider divides between two accounts when it settles.
SubaccountThe provider's name for a charity's own payout account within Givezy's merchant account.
Settlement / depositA payout from the payment provider into a bank account.
ChargebackA donor disputes a card payment with their bank and the money is taken back, pending the provider's and the merchant's answer.
Row-level securityA database feature that stops a user reading rows they should not, enforced by the database whatever the website code does.
Second stepA one-time code from an authenticator app, required on top of the emailed sign-in code for charity and platform administrators.
WebhookA message the payment provider sends to our server to say a payment succeeded, failed, was refunded or was disputed.
Tax year1 March to the end of February, named here by the year it ends (2026/27 is 01/03/2026 to 28/02/2027; stored as 2027).
